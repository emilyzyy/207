## Question 1 Response

### Principle 1: Equitable Use

- Colourblind users' equitable access is considered through implementation of category symbols (picture icons for Food/Shopping/Nature, etc.) rather than just the colour-coding
- Elderly/less technical users' questions about the app's use can be answered by George, who ensures clarity and simplicity in responses

### Principle 2: Flexibility in Use

- Different ways of adding activities to schedule are implemented, such as inputting start/end times and dragging/dropping activity cards
- Flexible planning flow: users can add activities to plan directly from search tab, bookmark many and add to plan from the bookmark tab instead, or add all of the activities they are interested in and autoschedule them.
- Flexibility for light-sensitivity preferences through light/dark mode.

### Principle 3: Simple and Intuitive Use

- Clear labels presented in a predictable workflow of search to bookmark to day plan is presented
- UI is made intuitive by recreating familiar map, calendar, and schedule layouts from popular apps
- Simplicity is maintained through minimal unnecessary information. Ex: notifications of deletion are cleared soon after they pop up.

### Principle 4: Perceptible Information

- Colour-coding activities by category and providing icons (mentioned above) for visually contrasting category information and preserving perceptibility for colour-blind users.
- George's icon and purpose is made apparent by a text box that encourages the user to chat with him. The size of the text box and George's icon are optimized to be very findable but not block nearby buttons (placed directly above Calendar View button when in day plan tab to avoid this specifically).

### Principle 5: Tolerance for Error

- Confirmation popups for deleting activities from day plan or bookmarks and deleting trips
- Overlap detection and validation of start/end times being within day trip limits when scheduling activities
- Notifications that explain scheduling conflicts and signify deletion of activities
- Cancel buttons (that are focused initially) that close dialogs without saving

### Principle 6: Low Physical Effort

- Autoschedule and drop down menus for inputting times rather than typing times to schedule activities, with drag-and-drop functionality.
- Search filters that cut down scrolling effort.

### Principle 7: Size and Space for Approach and Use

- Interfaces that remain usable at different window sizes.
- Text size optimized for readability, button size and spacing optimizing for clarity of actions, and activity cards that do not clip their actions.

## Question 2 Response

Trippy would be marketed toward leisure travellers as a whole. This is a vague and broad category, made up of many minorities whose needs must be addressed in the design to avoid tangible/relational harm from being unable to participate in trips. An important subset of leisure travellers are those who are on business trips planning leisure days/half-days. These users are accommodated by the flexibility of planning features since autoschedule allows time to be blocked off of day plans. Other subsets are group planners and older/less technically inclined users, whose needs are covered by Trippy's sharing and account-based saving features and AI chatbot/autoscheduling assistance, respectively. Despite the targeted audience being leisure travellers as a whole, user diversity is considered to ensure equitable accessibility.

## Question 3 Response

Currently, Trippy is less likely to be used by those with visual or motor impairments. Presently, the interface is purely visual and insufficient support for keyboard navigation. As such, there is large room for improvement in these areas under the social model of disability to prevent tangible/relational harm to these users. Possibilities include text-to-speech features and adding agentic behaviour to George to circumvent the need for users to manually manipulate schedules and timeframes. The majority-user assumption needs to be combatted in order to address these impaired minorities and provide universally accessible app functionality to the diverse userbase of travellers.
